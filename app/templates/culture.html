The Royal Tombs of Kasubi

The burial grounds of four Kabakas (Kings) of Buganda. Built entirely from wood, thatch and reed, it is a masterpiece of Ganda architecture and remains a living spiritual centre for the Baganda people.

Artisan Spotlight

The Legacy of
Bark Cloth

Recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, bark cloth making is an ancient craft of the Baganda people, predating the invention of weaving.

The process involves harvesting the inner bark of the Mutuba tree without destroying it — an early masterclass in sustainable harvesting. The bark is meticulously beaten with wooden mallets to create a soft, terracotta-colored fabric historically reserved for royalty.

verified Inscribed on the UNESCO list of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2005
eco

100% regenerative process. The Mutuba tree regrows its bark within a year, sustaining the craft indefinitely.

Traveling with Respect

Engaging with communities is a privilege. Mmondo promotes a model of cultural exchange built on dignity, mutual respect, and economic equity.

01

Photography Protocol

Always seek explicit permission before photographing individuals, particularly elders and children. Put the camera away first; connect human-to-human before documenting.

02

Economic Equity

Support local artisans directly. We discourage bargaining heavily for crafts, as these pieces represent hours of skilled labor and primary livelihoods.

03

Dress & Decorum

When visiting rural communities or sacred sites, conservative attire is appreciated. Observe local greetings and gestures as guided by your Mmondo expert.
